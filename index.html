How it works

Three steps, about one cup of tea.

  1. 1

    Format the card and write Raspberry Pi OS

    Put an SD card in your computer and open the free Raspberry Pi Imager. Pick Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit), then click Skip customisation: don’t set a name, user or Wi-Fi there, because the wizard does that. Imager formats the card as it writes.

  2. 2

    Run the wizard on the same computer

    When Imager has finished, open the wizard with the card still in your computer. If Imager ejected it, take it out and put it back in. Give your callsign, Wi-Fi, hat and frequencies, and the wizard sets up DMRHost on the card. Installing the dmrdash dashboard is your choice.

  3. 3

    Plug in the Pi and go make tea

    The first boot takes 5 to 15 minutes. You don’t have to watch, but here’s what it does:

    1. OKInstalls BrandMeister DMRHost
    2. OKSets up your hat, frequencies and OLED
    3. OKInstalls the dmrdash dashboard, if you chose it
    4. OKWipes your passwords off the card
    5. ON AIRThe OLED lights up. You’re on BrandMeister.
Dashboard tour

See who is talking, and change talkgroups, from your phone.

Once it is running, open http://<hotspot name>.local:8080 on any phone or computer on your network.

1 2 3 4 5 6
  1. 1Status light. One light for your hotspot software, radio board and BrandMeister link. Green is good. Tap it for the details and controls.
  2. 2Who is talking. The panel turns red when you transmit, and cream when it is someone else.
  3. 3Where. See the talkgroup and its name, with the speaker’s name below.
  4. 4Stop call. Cut off a conversation you do not want to hear on your hotspot.
  5. 5Your talkgroups. Pick the ones you always want to hear per timeslot. The API key you added in the wizard lets you change these without logging in to BrandMeister.
  6. 6Last heard. See everyone recently on the air, with links to look them up on QRZ.com.
  • Made for phones: it is readable at arm’s length while you hold the radio in your other hand.
  • Presets: save sets like “Everyday” or “Net night” and switch them with one tap. Only the difference is changed.
  • Timed statics: set your weekly net’s talkgroup to turn on only when the net meets, entered in local time.
  • Help with reception: if your audio is choppy, it suggests a better RX offset and tunes it while you key up, then asks before keeping it.
  • Clear warnings: a red banner appears when something is wrong, and it stays quiet when things are fine.
What you need

Your hotspot shopping list

Already run a hotspot? Your Pi and MMDVM_HS hat work as they are.

A Raspberry Pi

You will need a Zero 2 W, 3 or 4. The Zero 2 W is small, cheap, and runs most hotspots. The Pi 5 is not supported yet.

An MMDVM_HS hat

This small radio board plugs onto the Pi’s pins. You can choose simplex (one frequency) or duplex (two). An OLED screen is a helpful extra.

An SD card, 8 GB or more

You also need a way to plug it into your computer.

A BrandMeister account

Set a hotspot security password in SelfCare first. This is different from your login password, and the wizard shows you where to find it. You will also need a SelfCare API key to manage talkgroups from your dashboard.

A DMR radio and a DMR ID

Your ID comes from radioid.net. Just type your callsign into the wizard and it finds the ID for you.

Your amateur radio licence

A hotspot is a transmitter. Pick frequencies that your licence and local band plan allow.

Add a channel using the wizard’s numbers.

The hotspot’s receive frequency is your radio’s transmit frequency, and vice versa. The wizard shows both from your radio’s perspective, so you can copy them straight into your codeplug.

DMR explained in five terms

Talkgroup (TG)
A shared channel on the network. TG 91 is worldwide, and most countries have their own.
Timeslot (TS1, TS2)
DMR fits two conversations onto one frequency. A simplex hat uses TS2 only.
Colour code (CC)
This works like a CTCSS tone for DMR. The radio and hotspot must match; 1 is fine.
Static talkgroup
This channel is always on, so you hear it without keying up. The dashboard sets these for you.
Dynamic talkgroup
This turns on when you key up, and times out when the channel goes quiet.

Copy five numbers, then swap your cards.

Start with a new SD card and keep your current one safe. Copy these details from your current hotspot into the wizard. Your radio’s codeplug stays exactly the same.

  1. Write Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) to the new card
  2. Run the wizard and type in your numbers
  3. Swap the cards and power up. Give it 5 to 15 minutes.
This card suits you if
  • You only use DMR, and strictly on BrandMeister
  • You change talkgroups often, especially before nets
  • You want a dashboard that reads easily on a phone
  • You prefer setting things up once on your computer
Stay on WPSD if
  • You use D-Star, YSF, P25, NXDN or M17
  • You connect to TGIF, FreeDMR or other DMR networks
  • Your hotspot is a Pi 5, which we do not support yet
  • You need proven software, as this project is still early

Try it out on a spare card first: your current card stays exactly as it is, so you can swap back anytime. WPSD and Pi-Star are separate projects and are not affiliated with this one.

Common questions

Do I need to know Linux?

No, you never have to open a terminal on the Pi. If you do like using SSH, it is available with your key or password.

What if I type something wrong?

Just write the card again with Imager and rerun the wizard. You can always change talkgroups and the RX offset later from the dashboard.

Nothing happened after 15 minutes

This is usually a Wi-Fi issue. Check your network name and password, or plug in an Ethernet cable on a Pi 3 or 4. Setup retries every minute, even across reboots, so it will carry on by itself once online.

The dashboard shows a red banner

This means the BrandMeister link is down. Check the hotspot security password you set in SelfCare, as it is different from your BrandMeister login password.

I hear others, but they cannot hear me

Check that your radio’s TX frequency matches the hotspot’s RX frequency, and that the colour codes match. If your audio breaks up, try using the RX offset tuner in the dashboard.

Is it safe to lend someone the SD card?

Do not lend it before the first boot. Until then, the card holds your hotspot, Wi-Fi and login passwords in plain text. The first boot moves them to secure files and deletes them from the card.

Does this cost anything?

No, it is free and open source under the MIT licence. DMRHost is BrandMeister’s software, and dmrdash is the dashboard.

Is the project finished?

It is early days. The settings it writes match a working hotspot, but the full card-to-first-boot process has not been run on real hardware yet. Reports are always welcome on GitHub.
