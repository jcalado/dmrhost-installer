What’s a hotspot?

A tiny repeater on your desk.

No repeater in range? A hotspot listens to your radio over a few metres of UHF and passes your voice to BrandMeister over the internet. Hams all over the world hear you on the same talkgroup.

  1. Your radioA DMR handheld or mobile
  2. UHF
  3. HotspotRaspberry Pi + MMDVM_HS hat. This is what we set up.
  4. INTERNET
  5. BrandMeisterThe worldwide DMR network
  6. TG 91
  7. Everyone elseOn hotspots and repeaters worldwide
What you need

The shopping list

A Raspberry Pi

Zero 2 W, 3 or 4. The Zero 2 W is the small, cheap one most hotspots use. The Pi 5 isn’t supported yet.

An MMDVM_HS hat

The little radio board that plugs onto the Pi’s pins. Simplex (one frequency) or duplex (two). A small OLED screen is a nice extra.

An SD card, 8 GB or more

And a way to plug it into your computer.

A BrandMeister account

With a hotspot security password set in SelfCare. It isn’t your login password; the wizard shows you where to set it.

A DMR radio and a DMR ID

Your ID comes from radioid.net. Type your callsign in the wizard and it finds it for you.

Your amateur licence

A hotspot is a transmitter. Pick frequencies your licence and band plan allow.

How it goes

Three steps, about one cup of tea.

  1. 1

    Write Raspberry Pi OS to the card

    With the free Raspberry Pi Imager. Pick Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) and click Skip customisation. The guide has a screenshot of every screen.

  2. 2

    Fill in the wizard

    Callsign, Wi-Fi, hat, frequencies. It opens in your browser and explains each field. You can do it while Imager is still writing.

  3. 3

    Plug in the Pi and go make tea

    The first boot takes 5 to 15 minutes. You don’t have to watch, but here’s what it does:

    1. OKInstalls BrandMeister DMRHost
    2. OKSets up your hat, frequencies and OLED
    3. OKInstalls the dmrdash dashboard
    4. OKWipes your passwords off the card
    5. ON AIRThe OLED lights up. You’re on BrandMeister.
On your radio

Add one channel. The wizard tells you what to type.

The hotspot’s receive frequency is your radio’s transmit frequency, and the other way round. The wizard shows both from your radio’s side, so you can copy them into your codeplug as they are.

Words you’ll meet

DMR in five words

Talkgroup (TG)
A shared channel on the network. TG 91 is worldwide; most countries have their own.
Timeslot (TS1, TS2)
DMR fits two conversations on one frequency. A simplex hat uses TS2 only.
Colour code (CC)
Like a CTCSS tone for DMR. Radio and hotspot must match; 1 is fine.
Static talkgroup
Always on, so you hear it without keying up. The dashboard sets these for you.
Dynamic talkgroup
Turns on when you key up on it, and times out when it goes quiet.

Questions

Do I need to know Linux?

No. You never open a terminal on the Pi. If you like SSH, it’s there, with your key or password.

What if I typed something wrong?

Write the card again with Imager and rerun the wizard. Talkgroups and the RX offset you can change later from the dashboard.

Nothing happened after 15 minutes

Usually the Wi-Fi: check the network name and password, or plug in Ethernet on a Pi 3 or 4. Setup retries every minute, even across reboots, so once the Pi is online it carries on by itself.

The dashboard shows a red banner

The BrandMeister link is down. Check the hotspot security password you set in SelfCare: it isn’t your BrandMeister login password.

I hear others, but they can’t hear me

Check that your radio’s TX frequency matches the hotspot’s RX, and that the colour codes match. If your audio breaks up, try the RX offset tuner in the dashboard.

Is it safe to lend someone the SD card?

Not before the first boot. Until then the card holds your hotspot, Wi-Fi and login passwords in plain text. The first boot moves them to files only the Pi’s administrator can read, and deletes them from the card.

Does it cost anything?

No. It’s open source under the MIT licence. DMRHost is BrandMeister’s software; dmrdash is the dashboard.

Is it finished?

It’s early. Each part is tested, and the settings it writes match a working hotspot’s, but the whole card-to-hotspot run hasn’t been done on real hardware yet. Reports are welcome on GitHub.
